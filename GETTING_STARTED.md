# Getting Started

Welcome to the What a Waste 3.0 Global Data Hack.

Whether you're a waste industry expert, data analyst, researcher, developer, student, environmentalist, or simply someone curious about the challenge of global waste management, we're delighted you're here.

This guide will help you get up and running.

## Step 1: Introduce Yourself

Head over to the Miro board, say hello, and tell the community a little about yourself.

Things you might want to share:

- Who you are
- Where you're from
- Your areas of expertise
- Topics you're interested in exploring
- Skills you're able to contribute
- What you'd like to learn from the hackathon

### Collaboration Board

https://miro.com/app/board/uXjVHkEMU74=/

## Step 2: Explore the Data

The core dataset for the hackathon is the World Bank's **What a Waste Global Database**.

### Dataset

https://datacatalog.worldbank.org/search/dataset/0039597/what-a-waste-global-database

Spend some time exploring the available data and thinking about questions that interest you.

Some examples:

- How do waste generation rates differ around the world?
- What factors appear to influence recycling performance?
- How might waste relate to wealth, urbanisation, education or health outcomes?
- What trends or anomalies stand out?

---

## Step 3: Discover Opportunities

You don't need to arrive with a project idea.

Use the Miro board to:

- Share ideas
- Discuss interesting findings
- Find collaborators
- Join existing teams
- Suggest additional datasets
- Capture insights and questions

Many of the best projects begin with a simple question.

---

## Step 4: Build Something Interesting

There are no restrictions on project type.

Possible outputs include:

- Data analysis
- Visualisations
- Dashboards
- Interactive maps
- Research reports
- Storytelling projects
- Data integrations
- Forecasting models
- Policy recommendations
- Circular economy insights

Feel free to explore whatever aspect of the data excites you most.

---

## Step 5: Share Your Progress

As your project develops, document your work in this repository.

We recommend including:

- Project summary
- Team members
- Data sources used
- Methodology
- Findings
- Visualisations
- Links to any tools, notebooks or dashboards

Remember that the goal isn't just to produce something impressive. It's to learn, collaborate, and generate new insights from the data.

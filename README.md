# What a Waste 3.0 Global Data Hack

Welcome to the What a Waste 3.0 Global Data Hack.

This hackathon brings together waste professionals, data analysts, environmentalists, developers, students, researchers and curious minds from around the world to explore the data behind the World Bank's *What a Waste 3.0* report and uncover new insights into one of the world's most pressing challenges.

## The Challenge

The world generated **2.6 billion tonnes of waste in 2022**. Without action, this is projected to increase by approximately **50% by 2050**.

Waste is more than just what we throw away. It represents lost resources, missed economic opportunities, environmental harm and impacts on human health.

The rich collection of datasets released alongside the What a Waste 3.0 report provides an unprecedented opportunity to better understand global waste generation and management, connect it with other datasets and develop smarter, more circular solutions.

## About the Hackathon

This global hackathon is being delivered ahead of **ISWA World Congress 2026** in London.

The opening session will include an introduction to the What a Waste 3.0 report from **Ed Cook**, plastic pollution and waste management specialist and lead author of the report.

Participants will form teams, develop projects over the hack period and share their findings. Selected projects will be showcased as part of activities surrounding ISWA World Congress 2026.

## Who Should Join?

Everyone.

You do **not** need to be:

- A waste expert
- A data scientist
- A software developer
- An experienced hackathon participant

If you have curiosity, enthusiasm and an internet connection, you're welcome.

We encourage participation from:

- Waste and recycling professionals
- Environmental practitioners
- Analysts and data enthusiasts
- Researchers and students
- Software developers
- Policy professionals
- Anyone interested in sustainability and the circular economy

## Possible Project Ideas

Need inspiration? Here are a few possibilities:

- Analyse a particular aspect of the What a Waste 3.0 dataset
- Compare waste trends across cities, regions or countries
- Explore the relationship between waste and economic development
- Link waste data with air quality, health, education or demographic datasets
- Build visualisations and dashboards
- Create forecasting or modelling tools
- Identify opportunities for circular economy interventions

These are only suggestions. The most exciting projects are often the ones nobody has thought of yet.

## Explore a project

The [Shiny Waste Data project](projects/shiny-waste-data/README.md) is an
interactive R Shiny prototype for exploring the What a Waste 3.0 data. Try
the [live demo](https://what-a-waste.shinyapps.io/shiny-waste-data/), run it
locally, or jump into the code and experiment. It is a hackathon project:
questions, small improvements, and clearly documented explorations are
welcome. Its README explains how to get started and its
[AI contribution guidance](projects/shiny-waste-data/llms.txt) explains how
AI assistants can help with that project.
